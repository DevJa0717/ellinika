LESSON-WRITING BRIEF (C1 reading lessons for a personal Greek-learning web app)
Learner: Devon, English speaker, experienced polyglot, grammar ~B1-B2, vocabulary weaker, wants spontaneous intellectual conversation in Greek. Keep explanations brisk, no hand-holding.

FORMAT: write each lesson as /mnt/project-files/app/lessons/<id>.json (NEW files only; never edit any other file in app/). Model your JSON exactly on /mnt/project-files/app/lessons/c1-learned-verbs.json and c1-hedging.json (read them first). Top-level keys: "el" (Greek title), "title" (English title), "section" ("C1 · reading" or "C1 · exam practice"), "goal" (one sentence), "blocks" (list), "practice" (list).
Block types the app renders (only these): 
- {"type":"text","title"?,"text"} — markdown supports only **bold** and *italic*; paragraphs separated by \n\n.
- {"type":"callout","text"}
- {"type":"culture","title","text"}
- {"type":"table","title"?,"head":[...],"rows":[[...]]}
- {"type":"examples","title"?,"items":[{"el","en"}]}
- {"type":"reading","title","text","en","gloss":{"greek word form as it appears":"English"},"questions":[{"prompt","answer","options":[4 strings incl. answer],"why"}]}
  Each reading block becomes one swipe page; split long texts into 2-4 reading blocks of ~150-350 words at paragraph boundaries. "en" = your own accurate, natural English translation of exactly that chunk. gloss: 8-20 C1-level words per block (keys must match the word form in the text). questions: 2-3 per block, in Greek, testing real comprehension (inference, gist, attitude), distractors plausible but clearly wrong; the answer string must be one of options.
Practice items (array): {"gen":"mc","n"?,"items":[{"prompt","answer","options","why"}]} and {"gen":"type","n"?,"items":[{"prompt","answer","accept"?,"why"?}]}. Reading-block questions are added to practice automatically, so practice should hold: (1) a "type" set of 10-15 key words/phrases from the texts as English→Greek production ("Say in Greek: **to undermine**" → "υπονομεύω"), with accept lists for legitimate variants; (2) an "mc" set of 6-10 vocabulary-in-context or usage items. Prompts may mix Greek and English.
Each lesson: a short "text" intro block (what the text is, why it matters, what to watch for: register, learned forms, structure), the reading blocks, a "culture" block with genuinely useful background, and where useful a "table" of key vocabulary or of learned forms found in the text (e.g. katharevousa form → modern form).
Source texts: /mnt/project-files/data/c1/texts/<id>.json (fields text, title, author, url, license). Use the text verbatim (you may cut at paragraph boundaries and mark cuts with […]). Put attribution at the end of the intro text: *Πηγή: author, title (license).* For opinion pieces (copyrighted, personal use) keep the excerpt as is.
Accuracy: Greek must be correct modern monotonic orthography (unless quoting a polytonic original, then also give a monotonic version). Double-check every question against the text.
