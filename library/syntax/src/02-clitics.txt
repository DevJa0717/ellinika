title: Weak pronouns in the sentence

blurb: Where με, σου, τον, το, της go: before the verb, after commands and gerunds, in pairs, with θα, να and δεν, doubling a noun, and the accent they can add.

The weak (clitic) pronouns **με, σε, τον, την, το, μας, σας, τους, τις, τα** (accusative) and **μου, σου, του, της, μας, σας, τους** (genitive) are the most frequent words in Greek after the article. They have no stress of their own and must lean on a verb. Their position is fixed by strict rules, which this guide sets out one by one, so that you can produce them without thinking.

## A2 | Πριν από το ρήμα | Before the verb

With every ordinary verb form (present, past, future, subjunctive, perfect), the weak pronoun goes **immediately before the verb**.

> Σε ξέρω. || I know you.
> Τον είδα χθες. || I saw him yesterday.
> Μου αρέσει. || I like it.
> Της έγραψα ένα γράμμα. || I wrote her a letter.

Nothing can come between the pronoun and the verb except other weak pronouns. The little words **θα, να, ας, δεν, μην** go **before** the pronoun:

| Word | + pronoun + verb | Meaning |
|-|-|-|
| θα | Θα σε δω αύριο. | I'll see you tomorrow. |
| να | Θέλω να τον ρωτήσω. | I want to ask him. |
| ας | Ας το κάνουμε. | Let's do it. |
| δεν | Δεν το ξέρω. | I don't know it. |
| μην | Μην το πεις! | Don't say it! |
| δεν θα | Δεν θα σου το πω. | I won't tell you (it). |
| να μην | Να μην τους ξεχάσεις. | Don't forget them. |

In the perfect tenses the pronoun goes before **έχω**: **Το έχω δει** "I have seen it", **Σας είχαμε γράψει** "We had written to you".

? "I don't know him." | Δεν ξέρω τον. | *Δεν τον ξέρω. | Τον δεν ξέρω. || δεν, then the pronoun, then the verb.
? "I'll call you." | Σε θα πάρω. | *Θα σε πάρω. | Θα πάρω σε. || θα always comes before the weak pronoun.
~ "I have seen it already." | Το έχω δει ήδη. | Ήδη το έχω δει. || In the perfect, the pronoun goes before έχω.
= "We'll wait for you." (εσένα) => Θα σε περιμένουμε. || θα + σε + verb.

## A2 | Μετά την προστακτική και τη μετοχή | After commands and the gerund

There are only two forms after which the weak pronoun **follows** the verb: the **imperative** and the **gerund** (-οντας/-ώντας).

> Πες μου. || Tell me.
> Δώσε το! || Give it!
> Φέρ' τα εδώ. || Bring them here.
> Ρώτησέ τον. || Ask him.
> Βλέποντάς τον, κατάλαβα. || Seeing him, I understood.

A negative command is not an imperative but **μην + subjunctive**, so the pronoun goes back before the verb: **Πες το!** "Say it!" but **Μην το πεις!** "Don't say it!"

| Positive | Negative |
|-|-|
| Κάλεσέ τους. "Invite them." | Μην τους καλέσεις. "Don't invite them." |
| Γράψε μου. "Write to me." | Μη μου γράψεις. "Don't write to me." |
| Πάρε το. "Take it." | Μην το πάρεις. "Don't take it." |

The polite or soft command with **να** behaves like any subjunctive: **Να μου γράψεις!** "Do write to me!"

? "Tell him!" | Του πες! | *Πες του! | Πες τον! || After an imperative the pronoun follows; "tell someone" takes the genitive του.
? "Don't tell him!" | Μην πες του! | *Μην του πεις! | Μην του πες! || Negative commands use μην + subjunctive, with the pronoun before the verb.
= "Bring it!" (φέρε + το) => Φέρ' το! | Φέρε το! || The pronoun follows the imperative; φέρε often elides to φέρ'.
= "Don't bring it!" => Μην το φέρεις! || μην + το + subjunctive φέρεις. Μην keeps its ν before τ.

## A2 | Δύο αδύνατοι τύποι μαζί | Two weak pronouns together

When a verb has two weak pronouns, the **genitive** (indirect object, "to whom") comes **first**, the **accusative** (direct object, "what") second. This holds before and after the verb.

| Before the verb | After an imperative |
|-|-|
| Μου το έδωσε. "He gave it to me." | Δώσε μου το. "Give it to me." |
| Σου τα είπα. "I told you (them)." | Πες μου τα. "Tell me (them)." |
| Θα του το στείλω. "I'll send it to him." | Στείλ' του το. "Send it to him." |
| Μην της το πεις. "Don't tell her." | Πες της το. "Tell her." |

The accusative pair partner is almost always a third person: **το, τα, τον, την, τους, τις**. "He introduced me to her" is not ***μου με σύστησε** but **Με σύστησε σ' αυτήν** (strong pronoun with σε).

! After the imperative, colloquial Greek also says **Δώσ' το μου** with the accusative first. Both orders are heard; **δώσε μου το** is the safe one to use.

? "She told it to us." | Το μας είπε. | *Μας το είπε. | Μας είπε το. || Genitive first, accusative second, both before the verb.
? "Show them to me." (the photos) | Δείξε τις μου. | *Δείξε μου τις. | Μου τις δείξε. || After the imperative: genitive then accusative.
~ "I'll send it to you tomorrow." | Θα σου το στείλω αύριο. | Αύριο θα σου το στείλω. || θα + genitive + accusative + verb.
= "Don't give it to him!" => Μην του το δώσεις! || μην + του + το + δώσεις.

## B1 | Ο τόνος που προστίθεται | The extra accent

A Greek word cannot carry its stress further back than the third syllable from the end. When a weak pronoun is added after a word stressed on its **third-from-last** syllable, or two pronouns are added after a word stressed on its **second-from-last**, the stress would fall too far back, so the word gets a **second accent** on its last syllable.

| Word | + pronoun(s) | Written |
|-|-|-|
| διάβασε (stress on 3rd from end) | + το | διάβασέ το |
| ρώτησε | + τον | ρώτησέ τον |
| βλέποντας | + τον | βλέποντάς τον |
| το αυτοκίνητο | + μου | το αυτοκίνητό μου |
| δώσε (stress on 2nd from end) | + μου + το | δώσε μού το |
| φέρε | + μου + τα | φέρε μού τα |

When the word is stressed on its last two syllables there is no change: **πες μου, δώσε μου, το σπίτι μου, ο φίλος μου**.

! Without the second accent the clitic can be mistaken for a word of its own: **ο φίλος μου είπε** "my friend said" versus **ο φίλος μού είπε** "the friend told me". Here the accent on μού marks it as belonging to the verb. This is the only time a weak pronoun is written with an accent before the verb.

? "Read it!" | Διάβασε το! | *Διάβασέ το! | Διαβάσε το! || διάβασε is stressed on the third syllable from the end, so adding το gives a second accent: διάβασέ το.
? "Bring them to me!" | Φέρε μου τα! | *Φέρε μού τα! | Φέρε μοῦ τα! || Two pronouns after a word stressed on the second-from-last syllable: extra accent on the first pronoun.
= Add the possessive μου: **το τηλέφωνο** => το τηλέφωνό μου || Stress on the third-from-last syllable, so a second accent goes on the last syllable.
= Add **τον** to the gerund **ακούγοντας** => ακούγοντάς τον || The gerund is stressed on the third-from-last syllable before the pronoun.

## B1 | Ο κλιτικός διπλασιασμός | Clitic doubling

Greek often uses a weak pronoun **together with** the noun it stands for. This is not a mistake: it marks the noun as a topic (see the word-order guide).

> Τον Γιάννη τον ξέρω χρόνια. || Giannis, I've known him for years.
> Το είδες το μήνυμα; || Did you see it, the message?
> Της το είπα της Ελένης. || I told Eleni.
> Στη Μαρία της αρέσει το κρασί. || Maria likes wine.

With **αρέσει, λείπει, φαίνεται** and other verbs whose experiencer is in the genitive, doubling is almost obligatory when the person is a full noun: **Στον Νίκο του αρέσει η θάλασσα** "Nikos likes the sea". Without **του** the sentence sounds incomplete in speech.

With strong pronouns (**εμένα, εσένα, αυτόν**) the weak form is required as well: **Εμένα με ξέχασες** "You forgot *me*", **Σ' εμένα μου το είπε** "He told *me*".

| Without doubling | With doubling |
|-|-|
| Ξέρω τον Γιάννη. (neutral) | Τον Γιάννη τον ξέρω. (Giannis = topic) |
| Είπα την αλήθεια στη Μαρία. | Στη Μαρία της είπα την αλήθεια. |
| (impossible) *Εμένα ξέχασες. | Εμένα με ξέχασες. |

? "*Me*, he didn't invite." | Εμένα δεν κάλεσε. | *Εμένα δεν με κάλεσε. | Με εμένα δεν κάλεσε. || A strong pronoun needs its weak form next to the verb.
? "My mother likes the theatre." (spoken Greek) | Η μητέρα μου της αρέσει το θέατρο. | *Στη μητέρα μου της αρέσει το θέατρο. | Της μητέρας μου αρέσει το θέατρο το. || The experiencer of αρέσει is σε + accusative (or genitive), doubled by της.
~ "Giannis? I've known him for years." | Τον Γιάννη τον ξέρω χρόνια. || Fronted object, then its doubling pronoun before the verb.

## B2 | Αλυσίδες ρημάτων: κάθε αντωνυμία στο ρήμα της | Verb chains: each pronoun stays with its own verb

In a chain such as "I want to see it", the pronoun belongs to the verb whose object it is, and stays **inside** that verb's clause. Greek never moves it up to the first verb, as Italian or Spanish can.

> Θέλω να το δω. || I want to see it. (not *Το θέλω να δω)
> Μπορείς να μου το δώσεις; || Can you give it to me?
> Πρέπει να τους ενημερώσουμε. || We have to inform them.
> Άρχισα να τη συμπαθώ. || I began to like her.

If both verbs have an object, each takes its own pronoun: **Τον παρακάλεσα να μου το στείλει** "I asked him to send it to me" (τον belongs to παρακάλεσα, μου το to στείλει).

The same rule holds with **για να, πριν να, χωρίς να**: **Το έκανα για να σε βοηθήσω**, **Έφυγε χωρίς να μας χαιρετήσει**.

? "I can't find it." | Δεν το μπορώ να βρω. | *Δεν μπορώ να το βρω. | Δεν μπορώ το να βρω. || The pronoun belongs to βρω, so it goes after να.
~ "I asked him to send it to me." | Τον παρακάλεσα να μου το στείλει. || τον with παρακάλεσα; μου το with στείλει.
= "We have to tell her." (λέω, της) => Πρέπει να της το πούμε. | Πρέπει να της πούμε. || The weak pronoun follows να and goes before πούμε.

## C1 | Λεπτά σημεία | Fine points

- **The old order survives in set phrases and dialects.** Folk songs and fixed phrases sometimes put the pronoun after an ordinary verb (**Δόξα σοι ο Θεός**), and Cypriot Greek still does so in everyday speech (**Είδα τον** "I saw him"). In standard Greek this order is wrong outside the imperative and the gerund.
- **Two clitics, both accusative, are impossible.** "He made me a doctor" uses a full noun: **Με έκανε γιατρό**.
- **Formal writing avoids doubling** but keeps clitics with the genitive experiencer: **Στους πολίτες δεν τους δόθηκε καμία εξήγηση** sounds journalistic; a report would write **Δεν δόθηκε καμία εξήγηση στους πολίτες**.
- **The genitive clitic as a possessive in a verb phrase**: **Μου χάλασε το αυτοκίνητο** "My car broke down (on me)", **Του πέθανε η μάνα του** "His mother died (and he lost her)". The clitic shows the person affected; English adds "on me".
- **Ethic genitive in commands and wishes**: **Να μου είσαι καλά!** "Be well (for my sake)!", **Πού μου πας;** "Where are you off to?" This **μου** adds warmth and is typical of affectionate speech.

> Μου χάλασε το πλυντήριο. || My washing machine broke down on me.
> Να μου προσέχεις! || Look after yourself for me!
> Δεν δόθηκε καμία εξήγηση στους πολίτες. || No explanation was given to the citizens.

? What does **μου** add in **Μου έκλεψαν το πορτοφόλι**? | It means "my" only, so it must be removed. | *It shows I am the person affected: "They stole my wallet (from me)". | It is the subject. || The genitive clitic marks the person affected by the event, often with no need for "my".
? Which is a formal report's version? | Στους πολίτες δεν τους δόθηκε εξήγηση. | *Δεν δόθηκε εξήγηση στους πολίτες. | Δεν τους δόθηκε στους πολίτες εξήγηση. || Formal writing prefers the plain order without doubling.
